Start Here · In Plain English

The whole project in three simple pictures

No neuroscience degree needed. Here is what sleep replay is, why glowing brain movies fool older tools, and how Shift Happens keeps the answer honest.

1 · The Idea Sleep Replay

What is sleep reactivation?

  • When an animal explores or learns, a small group of brain cells fires together like a chord on a piano.
  • During deep sleep afterward, that same group of cells often fires together again.
  • Scientists compare sleep after the experience with sleep before it to see if the replay is real.
In short: the brain practices today's patterns during tonight's sleep.
2 · The Problem Slow Glow

Why do standard tools break?

  • Older replay detectors were built for sharp electrical ticks recorded with tiny wires.
  • Newer microscopes film thousands of brain cells glowing with calcium light, but that glow fades slowly over about a second.
  • Because frames blur into the next frame, old shortcuts mistake random noise for real memory replay.
In short: slow glow makes random chance look like a fake pattern.
3 · The Toolbox Fair Test

What does Shift Happens do?

  • It slides each cell's timeline by a random amount 500 times, keeping the slow glow intact while breaking real teamwork.
  • It puts four standard detectors through fake recordings where the right answer is already known.
  • Any detector that fails the practice test loses its vote - and if too few qualify, the toolbox reports Not enough evidence.
In short: test the ruler before trusting the measurement.

01 · The Open Problem

Why spike-era reactivation tests cannot be trusted blindly on calcium data

When an animal experiences a task, groups of neurons fire together. If those co-activity patterns reappear during subsequent NREM sleep (POST) more strongly than during sleep beforehand (PRE), neuroscientists call it reactivation.

Electrodes (Spikes)

  • Sparse, fast action potentials
  • Dozens of cells per session
  • Standard methods built here

Two-Photon Calcium

  • Thousands of cells (6,920 in RIKEN)
  • Slow ~1 s fluorescence decay
  • Autocorrelation breaks spike bounds
"A systematic review and analysis of this possibility [firing-rate confound] is still missing in the field ... a systemic variation of bin size and its impact on pattern detection is lacking." Tingley & Peyrache (2020), Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B 375: 20190231, §3b-v (quoted in research/briefing.md)

Even on spike data, methods often disagree on the same recording. Before asking whether a mouse calcium session shows sleep reactivation, the toolbox first asks: which methods actually work on calcium-like data?

02 · Approach & Pipeline

One shared circular-shift null, four standard methods, strict eligibility gates

The project name - Shift Happens - comes from the shared N1 null model: every neuron's trace is circularly shifted by its own random offset 500 times (seed = 0), strictly within one recording split.

Each neuron keeps its exact slow calcium autocorrelation, while cross-neuron synchrony is destroyed. One-sided p = (1 + count(null ≥ obs)) / 501 (floor 0.002, α = 0.05).

1 →

Import & Select

Load raw OASIS deconvolution (spike, 7.65 Hz) and NREM labels. Lock the top 300 active cells across both TASK bouts.

2 →

Eligibility Check

Verify cell counts, ≥300 NREM frames, and ≥5 TASK frames/cell for PCA/ICA. Failures are ineligible, never "no".

3 →

Run M1 - M4

Compute each method's single locked score and unit (POST − PRE) on identical splits with seeded streams.

4 →

Test vs. N1 Null

Run 500 circular shifts per method and split; report supplementary 20 s block-swap p-values alongside N1.

5 ✓

Referee & Verdict

Only methods that passed planted-truth controls get a vote. Needs ≥2 counted methods across both splits.

M1 · Cell-Pair Correlation Counted · Splits A & B

Do task cell-pair correlations match POST more than PRE?

Compares upper-triangle cell-pair correlation matrices across PRE, TASK, and POST.

D1 = r(C_TASK, C_POST) − r(C_TASK, C_PRE)
Controls: Passes all four planted-truth referee cases (a-d). Eligible in both RIKEN splits (≥300 NREM frames each).
M2 · Explained Variance (EV / REV) Disqualified · Failed Control

Does POST explain TASK variance beyond PRE?

Computes squared partial correlation EV (controlling for PRE) minus reversed REV.

D2 = EV − REV  (requires ≥2 independent sessions)
Controls: Gives 6/10 false "yes" when assemblies already exist in PRE (limit ≤2/10). Block-swap fixes it but drops positive control to 8/10 (<9/10).
M3 · PCA Reactivation Strength Counted · Split A Only

Do TASK principal-component chords sound louder in POST?

Extracts TASK components above the 95th-percentile N1 bound, then projects sleep onto them.

D3 = mean_k [ mean_t R_k(POST) − mean_t R_k(PRE) ]
Controls: Passes counted 50-seed tests (50/50 positive, 50/50 reversal). Split B is ineligible: 651 TASK frames < 1,500 (5 × 300 cells).
M4 · ICA Reactivation Strength Disqualified · Failed T-M4-1

Can ICA unmix overlapping assemblies before scoring?

Applies FastICA inside the significant PCA subspace (≥2 components) to separate overlapping groups.

D4 = mean_k [ mean_t R_k^ICA(POST) − mean_t R_k^ICA(PRE) ]
Controls: Fails locked overlap test T-M4-1 (seed 0: ICA 0.911, PCA 0.817 vs. <0.8 required; seeds 0-9 ICA ≥ 0.9 in 5/10). Threshold not moved.

04 · Real-Data Results

05 · Limits

Limits of this study

Confidence is high that this pipeline finds no reactivation on RIKEN mouse05 under the locked keys - and deliberately low that this single session generalizes to calcium sleep reactivation overall.

1

One animal, no learning task

Zenodo record 17667863 ships one sleep session (mouse05) without a behavioural learning task. A wake bout stands in for TASK, and Splits A and B are halves of the same recording.

2

Two methods failed their controls

M2 failed synthetic pre-existing assemblies (6/10 false "yes") and M4 missed T-M4-1 (PCA 0.817 vs. <0.8). Both remain strict documented failures in SUBMISSION.md.

3

Short Split B excluded M3 and M4

Split B contains 651 TASK frames, below the locked 1,500-frame minimum (5 × 300 cells), leaving M3 scorable only in Split A.

4

Internal sign-off and separate modules

PROBLEM.md was signed by the team lead without an external scientist, and toolbox/verdict.py and the referee tests run as separate modules alongside run.py.
